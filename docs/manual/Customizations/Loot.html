Rolled Loot (Season 3)

Season 3 replaces most fixed, hand-placed loot with items that are rolled when they drop: a rarity, a coloured name and random enchantments, scaled to where the item came from.

Being built now. The loot engine and rolled chests are on the test realm. Creature and boss drops, sockets and gems, and the loot wishlist are finished and reach the test realm together with the next build. The Shard of Fate comes last, and all of it lands before Season 3 opens. Each stage has its own section on this page.

Rarity tiers

Every rolled item has one of five rarities. The rarity sets the item's name colour, how many random enchantments it gets, how hard each one tries for the top of its range, its sockets and its gold ceiling. It does not make any single enchantment stronger than the loot level allows: see How an item is rolled.

  • Names. A rolled item is named after its first two enchantments, for example Burning Longsword of Warding.
  • Trade-offs. Some rolls carry a drawback, such as lower AC, a lower saving throw or a weakness to one element. An item with a drawback gets one extra enchantment and a higher value ceiling to pay for it, at any rarity.
  • Unidentified drops. About a third of rolled items drop unidentified. Identify them with Lore, the Rod of Fast Lore or the Party Loot Roll's Lore step to see the coloured name and the enchantments.
  • Named items. A Legendary roll sometimes awards one of the module's own named boss items instead of a random one.
  • New kinds of power. Rolled loot favours what the current endgame lacks: Spell Resistance, elemental immunities and resistances, saves and immunities on jewellery instead of more physical immunity, and caster bonuses on magic staves. It never rolls Haste, divine damage or immunity to critical hits.
  • The forge. A rolled item can be worked at a forge like any other piece, within that forge's caps. An item already worth more than a forge's value cap cannot be enchanted further there, the same as the existing top gear.

Loot levels

Every source of rolled loot has a loot level from 1 to 60. The loot level sets how strong the enchantments can be and the value ceiling for each rarity. A creature's loot level comes from its challenge rating:

Creature CRLoot level
up to 401–30
40–20030–45
200–1,00045–55
1,000 and above55–60

So easy places stay easy: farming low-level monsters or chests will not turn up high-level loot. A chest uses the loot level of the area it stands in, not the level of the player who opens it.

How an item is rolled

Every rolled item goes through the same steps, in this order. Apart from the item type (see the wishlist), nothing about your character changes the result.

  1. Rarity. The game draws a rarity by the odds in the rarity table. A source with a minimum rarity raises a lower draw to that minimum: a large chest is at least Uncommon, a boss at least Epic. Then a small chance bumps the result up one tier, or rarely two. The odds in the table already include those bumps.
  2. Named item. A Legendary roll sometimes awards one of the module's own named items instead. When it does, the steps below are skipped.
  3. Item type. The source picks a kind of gear (melee weapon, armour, ring and so on) by its own odds, then a base item of that kind. Your wishlist steers this step, and only this step.
  4. How many enchantments. The rarity sets the count, with every count in its range equally likely: an Epic item gets 3 or 4.
  5. A drawback? Each rarity has a small chance of one drawback (a penalty or a weakness, see the last table below). An item that gets a drawback also gets one extra enchantment, and its gold ceiling rises.
  6. Which enchantments. Each enchantment is drawn one at a time from the list for that kind of item, by the Share column in the tables below. An item never gets two enchantments of the same family: never both an enhancement and an attack bonus, never two saving-throw bonuses, never the same element twice. Some enchantments only appear from a minimum loot level.
  7. How strong each one is. Each enchantment's strength is rolled inside the range the tables show for the item's loot level, with every value in the range equally likely. Rare and Epic items roll twice and keep the better result, and Legendary items roll three times. The rarity never widens the range or lifts its top.
  8. The gold ceiling. The game prices the enchantments it picked. If they are worth more than the rarity's gold ceiling at that loot level, the last enchantment is weakened one step at a time, then the one before it, and so on. If that is still not enough, the last enchantment is removed. The first enchantment, which gives the item its name, gives ground last. The ceiling only ever takes power away: an item that comes in under it is not topped up.
  9. Sockets and identification. Last, the item gets its sockets (by rarity) and may drop unidentified.
Rarity adds enchantments. The loot level sets how strong they are. An Epic item and a Common item from the same loot level roll each enchantment from the same range. The Epic one gets three or four enchantments instead of one, and keeps the better of two rolls for each, but neither can go past the top of the range. For example, an Epic tower shield from a loot level 50 boss rolled universal saving throws (range +3 to +5, it got +4), arcane spell failure (range −20% to −40%, it got −30%) and 20% sonic immunity. Those enchantments were worth about 108,000 gp against an Epic ceiling of 850,000 gp, so the ceiling never came into it.
Known problem: damage immunities are lost. When a roll picks an acid, cold, electrical, fire, sonic or negative energy immunity, the immunity is currently dropped when the item is made, so the item arrives one enchantment short. The example shield lost its sonic immunity this way and arrived with only two enchantments. A fix is in hand. Until it lands, armour, shields, jewellery and other wearables will sometimes come up short. Elemental resistances are not affected.

  • From level is the lowest loot level that can roll it.
  • The level columns give the range a roll can land in at that loot level. Levels in between scale evenly. “—” means it cannot roll at that level yet, and “yes” marks an enchantment that has no strength.
  • Ranges are before the gold ceiling, which can only lower them.

Chests and containers

Every ordinary chest, barrel, crate and sack in the world now holds rolled loot instead of BioWare's stock treasure. The stock treasure stopped scaling at character level 17, so above that it was mostly junk.

  • The area sets the level. Each area has a loot level, taken from the typical challenge rating of the monsters that live there. A chest in the Bree Cave rolls at level 1 whoever opens it; a chest in the Ice Queen's Nest rolls at level 38. Towns and other places without monsters have a level chosen to match the region around them.
  • What a chest holds. A share of rolled gear, some gold, and a few consumables (potions, scrolls, trap and healer's kits, wands and ammunition, all scaled to the loot level). The gold sometimes comes as precious gems of the same value instead, which any merchant buys.
  • Small, medium and large chests. A small chest holds at most one piece of gear, a medium one up to two, and a large one up to three. A large chest's gear is always at least Uncommon. The few weapon-only and ranged-only chests roll only that kind of gear.
  • Respawning chests refill on the first open after about three and a half minutes, the same timer as before. Barrels and crates that you can smash open fill once, when first opened or broken.
  • The Setti coffers in Númenor: Noirinan, guarded by Summanus, are the top-tier chests. Each still pays out at most once per real hour, but now rolls one Legendary item of its own kind at Noirinan's loot level: a belt, a ring or a shield. Some of those Legendary rolls are one of the module's named items of that kind instead.
  • Unchanged: quest chests and their rewards, the Donations Chest, bookshelves and books.

Creature and boss drops

Monsters now drop rolled gear too, on top of whatever they always carried. The drop lands on the corpse, so in a party it goes through the usual Need / Greed roll. A kill only counts when a player helped make it: your own summons, and creatures killed by traps or other monsters, drop nothing extra.

  • Ordinary creatures drop a rolled item 4% of the time.
  • Elites drop one 25% of the time. A creature is an elite when it is clearly stronger than the place it lives in: its challenge rating's loot level is at least five above its area's.
  • Bosses on the Roll of the Fallen always drop: two items of at least Epic rarity (three from the hardest bosses), and sometimes one of the module's named items as well.
  • Shards of Fate. About one boss kill in ten also drops a Shard of Fate. It always goes through the party's Need / Greed roll, even though it is worth almost nothing in gold.
  • The level is the creature's own. A drop rolls at the loot level of the creature's challenge rating, not the area's and not yours, so a tougher monster drops better gear.

Champions. Now and then an ordinary monster spawns as a champion: its name gains "Elite", it glows red, and it has double the hit points and better attack, damage, armour class and saves than its kind. It drops like an elite, gives more experience, and counts in your bestiary as its ordinary kind, with a separate tally of the champions you have slain. Champions are commonest where elites are rare (about 1 spawn in 20) and rarer in places already full of elites (down to 1 in 100).

Loot wishlist

You can choose the kinds of gear you want to see more of. Open the rest menu and pick Loot wishlist...: a window lists every kind of item that can drop, grouped as melee weapons, ranged weapons, armour, shields, jewellery, other wearables and staves. Add up to five, for example longswords, tower shields and rings.

  • 40% of the gear rolled for you is one of the item types on your wishlist, whenever the source can drop that kind of item. A chest that only holds ranged weapons still gives ranged weapons. The wishlist chooses only the type of item. Its rarity and enchantments are rolled exactly as before.
  • Who the loot is rolled for: the player who opens a chest. For a creature's drop, one of the players who helped kill it and are still in the area, chosen at random. So in a party, everyone's wishlist gets its turn.
  • Change it any time out of combat, free. Your wishlist belongs to your character and is kept when you log out.
  • Auto. With an empty wishlist, the same 40% leans toward the weapons your feats favour: Weapon Focus, Weapon Specialization, Improved Critical, their epic versions and Weapon of Choice, more strongly the more of them you hold. With none of those feats, it leans toward the weapons you are proficient with. A spellcaster also leans toward magic staves, so a wizard sees more daggers, clubs, crossbows, quarterstaffs and magic staves. The window shows what Auto is doing for you, and its Auto button switches it off if you would rather have plain random loot.

Sockets

Gear can carry sockets: empty slots that take a socket gem. An item's sockets are listed at the end of its description, for example Sockets (1/2): Flawed Emberstone, [empty].

  • Rolled loot gets its sockets from its rarity: Common none, Uncommon 0–1, Rare 1, Epic 1–2, Legendary 2–3.
  • Existing gear gets sockets too: hand-made items, shop stock and the gear you already own. Every weapon, armour, shield, ring, amulet, belt, cloak, helmet and so on gets one socket, and the most valuable pieces get two. Plot items, cursed items and stacks (ammunition, throwing weapons) get none. Gear you carry gets its sockets when you log in; anything else gets them the first time you try to set a gem in it.
  • Sockets are not forge slots. An enchantment a gem adds does not use up one of the item's forge enchantment slots. Its value still counts towards a forge's value cap, like any other worth in the piece.
  • Socketing is permanent. A gem cannot be taken out again, and no forge will strike or rework an enchantment a gem gave or raised.

Socket gems

A socket gem is used from your pack: activate it and target an item in your inventory, worn or carried. The item must be identified and have an open socket. The gem is used up only when it takes; if it cannot be set, you keep it and are told why.

  • Gems raise, they never replace. Each gem belongs to one family and raises the item's own enchantment of that kind by a number of steps. If the item has none, the gem adds it. A Fire gem on a sword with 1d6 fire damage makes it 1d8, 1d10 or more, depending on the gem; a gem never lowers what the item already has.
  • Caps. A gem can raise elemental damage up to 2d10 and enhancement or attack bonus up to +16. Armour Class and ability bonuses stop at +10 and saving throws at +8. A gem that would not make the item any stronger is refused.
  • The damage steps are +1, +2, +3, +4, 1d6, 1d8, 1d10, 2d6, 2d8, 2d10. A flat +5 counts as better than 1d8, so one step takes it to 1d10.
  • Where gems fit. Damage gems go into melee weapons. Enhancement / attack gems go into weapons and magic staves (melee weapons get enhancement, ranged weapons attack bonus). Armour Class, ability and saving throw gems go into armour, shields, jewellery and other worn gear; Dexterity gems also fit ranged weapons, and Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma gems also fit magic staves.

Gem rarity. Every gem comes in five cuts, coloured like loot rarity: Chipped (Common), Flawed (Uncommon), Cut (Rare), Flawless (Epic) and Radiant (Legendary). A rarer cut adds more steps: a damage gem adds 1 to 5 steps, the others 1 to 3.

Where gems come from. Chests sometimes hold one (large chests most often), ordinary monsters drop one now and then, elites more often, and every boss on the Roll of the Fallen drops one. Easy places give the lower cuts: Radiant gems only drop at loot level 45 and above, and bosses never drop below a Cut gem where the area allows it.

Shard of Fate

A Shard of Fate is a rare crystal token with two uses. Either use spends it.

  • Reforge an item's fate. Put a piece of rolled loot on the anvil of a Forge of Wonders smith (Tagget, Kimli, Bellnius or the Kallrist forge), keep the Shard in your pack, and ask the smith to reforge its fate. The item's random enchantments are removed and new ones are rolled at the same loot level and rarity, so the result is never stronger than a fresh drop of that rarity could be. The item keeps its base type, look, sockets and description. Enchantments you added at a forge yourself, and gems set in its sockets, stay as they are, and the new enchantments never duplicate them. The smith asks you to confirm first, because a reforge cannot be undone.
  • What a reforge refuses. Items that were not rolled (quest rewards, store stock, hand-made gear) and the named items a Legendary roll can award have nothing random to change. An item whose forged enchantments already fill it, or that sits at the forge's value limit, may have no room for anything new. In every one of these cases the smith hands the Shard back.
  • The forge's limits still apply. A reforge never takes an item past the forge's value limit or enchantment count, unless the item was already past them before the reforge, and then it never grows.
  • A bonus roll on a boss. When a boss on the Roll of the Fallen dies and you helped to kill it, you are in the same area and you carry a Shard, a window asks whether to spend it. If you accept, one more item from that boss's loot (Epic rarity or better, at the boss's loot level) goes straight into your pack, without a party roll. You have 60 seconds to choose. If you keep the Shard or let the window close, nothing is spent.
  • Once a day per boss. Each character can take one bonus roll per boss every 24 hours. A boss you have already used a bonus roll on today does not ask again, and other bosses are unaffected.
  • Where Shards come from. About one boss kill in ten drops one, and the merit shop sells them for 3 merit. A Shard can be traded or given away, but no merchant will buy one.
