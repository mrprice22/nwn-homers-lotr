Customizations — Homer's LOTR

This module runs on stock Neverwinter Nights rules (plus CEP 2 content), but a number of spells, mechanics, and whole systems have been deliberately changed. This page is the full reference for what has — and has not — been modified relative to a vanilla NWN game, drawn directly from the module's scripts. A short, in-game version lives in your journal under Server Info → Module Customizations.

Looking for where to play rather than how the rules differ? See the Leveling Guide and the Quest Guide.

Looks Like a Bug — Isn't

A handful of things on this server look broken the first time you meet them and are working exactly as intended. If one of these just happened to you, you are not the first — follow the link for the full explanation.

A feat is listed twice on my character sheet, and I only picked it once
Expected for casters at class level 40 and above. One of the two is an inert stand-in that exists so the level-up screen stops offering you a feat you already own. You get the benefit once, and it can appear on its own without you picking anything. See Why a feat can show twice.
Level-up offers me no spells to choose past 40 — every tab is empty
That screen belongs to the game client and cannot be repaired from the server. Do not worry about it — just finish the level-up and wait a few seconds. A Spellbook Study window opens by itself and hands you every pick the screen ate. Close it and your picks are still banked; finish a rest and it opens again. See Spellbook Study.
My sorcerer or bard cannot swap known spells past 40
A known limitation of the same client screen, not something you have done wrong. You keep every spell you know and gain more casts per day of all of them. Tracked on the Roadmap — see Spellbook Study.
The boss healed itself and got stronger when I backed off
Deliberate, and only the unique bosses do it. Disengaging from one of them is meant to cost you. See Boss Enrage on Retreat.
Devastating Critical stopped killing things outright
The stock save-or-die was removed on purpose, for you and for everything you fight. It now lands as a very large hit instead. See Critical Hits & Devastating Critical.
I was healed and then could not be healed again for a while
Full-restore healing is a timed resource here rather than a button you can hold. See Soul-fatigue on Heal.
My shifted form shows the wrong attack and damage on the character sheet
The merge worked — the sheet just doesn't redraw for it. When you polymorph, your gear's magical properties are copied onto the form's natural attacks and hide, but the character sheet keeps showing the shape's own base numbers, so your attack bonus and damage look far lower than they are. Trust the combat log instead: roll a few attacks and you will see the real to-hit and the full damage breakdown, bonuses included. Nothing is lost and nothing needs re-equipping. See Shapeshifting: Full Equipment Merge.
I was turned to stone and the death window won't let me Respawn
Petrification here is a timed death, not an instant one. For the first couple of minutes you are a statue and the game's own death window shows Respawn greyed out — that is deliberate breathing room, and it is there for a reason: shout for help. Anyone nearby can still cure you with Stone to Flesh or a Restoration-family cure, and if they reach you in time the countdown is cancelled outright and you walk away alive. If nobody does, the stone comes off, you die properly, and the death window returns with Respawn enabled — from that point you can respawn at your bind point or be raised or resurrected like any other death. Chat and floating text count the time down for you the whole way.
An item I picked up vanished into a roll window
Nobody stole it. Valuable drops go to a Need / Greed / Pass roll when party members are nearby. See Party Loot Rolls.

Something not on this list and genuinely wrong? Report it — shipped bug reports earn Merit.

Notable Custom Systems

Larger persistent systems layered on top of the base game — the things you will interact with most.

Appraise & Selling to Merchants

A high Appraise skill raises the maximum gold a merchant will pay you for an item. Many shops cap how much they will hand over for any single piece; Appraise lifts that cap for you specifically — up to double (+100%) at an Appraise check of 65 (you always "take 20", so your Appraise skill + 20). The benefit is per-character and never shared with other shoppers. You need at least one point of Appraise before it helps; with none, you sell at the shop's normal cap. Merchants that don't buy from players still don't, and shops with no buy cap are unchanged.

Banking & Family Accounts

Bank NPCs (in Bree, Minas Tirith, and elsewhere) let you deposit and withdraw gold and XP. Beyond a personal balance, a family account is shared across every character on your account, so you can pool gold and pass items between your own characters. XP banking is capped so it cannot be used to over-level past the module's ceiling.

Merit Reward System

You earn merit points when your reported bugs get fixed, your reported exploits get closed, or your suggested features ship — defects are worth 1, features 2, and exploits 3. Points are tracked per account and never expire. Spend them from the rest menu (or with a DM) at the Keeper-of-Records, Barliman Butterbur, in the Prancing Pony. See the Roadmap & Player Ideas page for what has shipped and who earned merit for it.

What is boosted. The 2× multiplier applies to gold and XP you earn: killing creatures (kill XP and the gold they drop) and turning in quests. It never stacks beyond 2× — a personal boost and a server boost do not multiply together.

What is not boosted. Withdrawing gold or XP from a bank (the Bree gold bank, the family bank, or the XP bank) is not boosted — banking just moves value you already have, it does not create it. Selling items to merchants is not boosted either. (Separately, several merchants had their per-item buy cap doubled, so a genuinely valuable item can now fetch its full price — but the sell rate is unchanged.)

Queueing — boosts never overlap. Running two boosts of the same kind at once would waste one, so extra purchases queue instead of stacking:

  • Buy a second boost of a kind you already have and its time is held in reserve; it starts counting down only when the current one runs out.
  • A live server-wide boost pauses everyone's personal boost — you are already getting 2× from the server, so your personal time is frozen and resumes, undiminished, once the server boost ends.
  • Server boosts from different players queue into a single server-wide chain, one after another.

When you log in, the server tells you every active and pending boost — server-wide and your own — who bought each, and the estimated date the whole chain ends.

MeaningWave Philosopher Guides

Eight philosopher NPCs are scattered across Middle-earth. Answer each one's questions to unlock them as a powerful summonable companion that persists between sessions. See the dedicated MeaningWave: A Guide to the Guides for routes and details.

Instant Resting & the Rest Menu

Resting is fully custom. There is no rest cooldown — you can rest whenever you are not in active combat, and each rest is an instant full rest (HP and spells restored immediately, no waiting). Resting opens a custom rest menu that also lets you take other actions from the same place, including teleporting to key destinations and summoning henchmen and Legend companions.

Party Loot Rolls

When you pick up a valuable item and other party members are in the same area, a clickable Need / Greed / Pass window pops up for everyone in the party — a fair way to decide who gets the drop, with no out-of-character arguing. It is loosely modelled on the loot roll from MMOs.

  • Need beats Greed: the highest Need roll wins; if nobody needs it, the highest Greed roll wins. Pass bows out and can never win.
  • The finder rolls too and keeps the item if they win. Otherwise the item is handed to the winner automatically.
  • You have 30 seconds to choose; no answer counts as a Pass.
  • The window shows the item's type (e.g. Scimitar, Wand, Heavy Armor), its gold value, and a grouped list of its magical properties.

Unidentified items. If the item is not yet identified, every player who would roll first makes a Lore check (your Lore skill plus a d20 against a difficulty based on the item's value — the same rule the Rod of Fast Lore uses). The results are shown in your combat log. If anyone succeeds, the item is identified for the whole party. If nobody does, the popup reveals only the item's type and marks it Unidentified — its name, value, and properties stay hidden, so you roll on a mystery.

Configuring it (rest menu → Party loot roll settings).

  • Any player can opt out of party loot rolls (you will never be prompted, and rolls won't trigger just for you).
  • The party leader sets the minimum item value that triggers a roll (default 10,000 gp; choose 5k, 10k, 50k, 100k, 250k, 500k, or 1M), or disables party rolling entirely.
  • You get a green confirmation message of the current settings whenever you join a party, become a party leader, or the leader changes a setting.

Boss Respawn Tracker — the Roll of the Fallen

A billboard in the Well of Eru (next to the Recent Updates sign) that shows which of Middle-earth's great powers are currently slain and when each will walk again — so you can plan a boss run instead of hiking to an empty lair.

  • Only bosses that are dead right now are listed, sorted mightiest first (by challenge rating), with the time until it returns at the end of each name — e.g. The Mighty Smaug — returns in 12m 30s.
  • Click a name to see the boss's lair and the player or party who last slew it (party credit uses the same damage-contribution rules as the Bestiary).
  • Every unique boss is tracked — the board covers each of Middle-earth's named great powers (formally: any creature with a challenge rating above 60 that exists as a single, unique foe rather than a repeatable spawn).
  • The full list of tracked bosses is on the wiki under Creatures → Bosses — the page is generated from the very registry the game loads, so it always matches the board. Think a boss is missing? Suggest it on Discord.
  • A server restart revives every boss, so the board starts each session empty.

Respawn timing: most bosses return on a 15-minute timer, and the board shows a live countdown for each. A handful of lair bosses keep their own longer timers (shown accurately on the board). Every tracked boss now respawns on its own — none are stuck until a reboot.

Boss Enrage on Retreat

Retreating from a great boss makes it stronger. Disengage from one of the Roll of the Fallen bosses and it will taunt you by name, grow in power, and claw back part of the damage you had done. This applies only to those unique bosses — ordinary creatures you walk away from behave exactly as before.

  • You are "in the fight" from the moment you damage the boss — in melee, at range, with spells, or through your summons, animal companion or henchman (their damage counts as yours).
  • You have "retreated" if you leave the boss's area, move more than about 40 metres away, or log out — and stay that way for roughly 12 seconds. Landing another hit resets the count, so an archer or caster fighting from long range stays engaged as long as they keep contributing.
  • What the boss gains, each time: a shouted taunt naming you, +2 to all six ability scores for the rest of its life, and an instant heal of 25% of its missing HP.
  • The enrage stacks — every retreat is another +2 and another quarter-heal — and it cannot be dispelled.
  • It fires per player: if you run while the rest of your party keeps fighting, the boss still enrages.
  • Dying is not retreating. If the boss kills you, it gains nothing.
  • The enrage never carries onto a respawn — a boss that returns to its lair is back at its base statistics.
  • Leave it alone and it recovers. Once nobody is fighting it, a boss waits out its own respawn time (the countdown shown on the Roll of the Fallen board) and then fully resets: every enrage stack gone, back to full health, spells and abilities restored, back at its lair, and anything taken from it put back. It shouts when it happens. Landing a hit restarts that clock, so a boss is only ever "left alone" if you really have left it alone — and the party after you never inherits a half-dead, over-tuned boss.

Every retreat undoes a quarter of your progress and leaves you facing a stronger enemy than the one you ran from. The winning line against a great boss is a single sustained engagement, brought with the gear and build to see it through — not attrition in shifts.

This is the companion mechanic to Soul-fatigue on Heal: fatigue makes chain-healing lethal, and enrage removes the option of backing off to wait the stacks out. Design background: Endgame Difficulty — Enrage-on-retreat.

Caster Signature Gear

A planned line of boss-drop gear that boosts specific spells, giving Clerics, Druids, Sorcerers, and Wizards a real way to threaten high-tier bosses instead of relying on damage shields and lucky epic-spell rolls. Each piece pairs top-tier stats with one signature effect — amplifying a spell's damage, retyping it past an immunity, or supercharging your summons — and every item grants a stronger version to pure single-class casters.

  • Twenty items (five each per caster class), each looted from a thematically matched boss already in the world — see the full catalogue with stats and drop sources on the Caster Signature Gear page.
  • Builds on Scaled Summons (for the summon-boost pieces) and the same spell-effect machinery as Doubled Spell Duration.
  • The whole system uses one dedicated slot — the amulet. Only one signature effect can ever be active, and a second copy of the same effect does not stack.

First pieces built; nothing obtainable yet. The four Damage Retyper amulets — one per caster class — now exist in the module: they turn Implosion (Cleric), Finger of Death (Druid), Wail of the Banshee (Sorcerer) and Weird (Wizard) from a death effect that bosses simply ignore into real damage they have to survive. Where each one drops has not been decided, so none of them is placed in the world yet. The rest of the catalogue is still a preview and its numbers may change. Track it on the Roadmap.

Dungeon Solitaire

A custom card-game-style encounter arena: a sequence of drawn challenges with its own creatures and dialogue, distinct from the open-world encounter zones. It is an in-game port of Dungeon Solitaire — which can also be played stand-alone on any computer with the .NET framework.

Pipe-Weed & the Smoking Chamber

Through the door at the Well of Eru lies the Smoking Chamber, a low room of cushions and water pipes. Bring a pouch of Shire leaf, use a pipe, and the smoke settles on you for one full hour of real time.

Every strain is a genuine trade — a real gift bought with a real penalty — and, this being the point of the whole business, resting will not clear it. Both halves ride with you into the next fight, so choose your leaf before you set out rather than after. Logging out will not shake it either; the hour is counted in real time and picks up where it left off when you return. Lighting a second pipe replaces the first entirely — there is no stacking and no mixing.

Appearance & Crafting

Free cosmetic changes to your character and gear, plus paid enchanting at the Forge of Wonders.

Player Appearance & Alignment

Visit the Appearance Changer and Character Modifying Area in Bree to open an appearance menu with 315+ creature forms. The change is free and permanent until you change it again.

The same area also lets you change your alignment (good/evil and law/chaos). This is how you set up otherwise-impossible class combinations — shift your alignment to qualify for builds like a monk/barbarian or a paladin/blackguard.

Item Appearance Crafting

Armor, cloak, shield and weapon appearance can be remodelled with the CEP crafting system (the Craft Armor / Craft Weapon options). There is no fail chance — every appearance change succeeds automatically, regardless of your skill ranks. Reshaping the model of a piece (shoulders, boots, weapon parts, shield model, etc.) is reached from the Dye Studio via its Reshape appearance… button; recolouring is done in the Dye Studio itself.

Dye Studio — Recolour Your Gear

The Dye Studio is a point-and-click colour picker for your armor, helmet and cloak. It's free, instant, and can't fail, every colour is previewed live on your character, and dyeing never changes an item's stats.

Where to get it: buy a Dye Kit from the Well Mart in the Well of Eru, or at Tagget's Smith Shop in Bree. Keep it in your inventory and use (activate) it to open the Dye Studio window.

The window opens beside your character so you can watch the preview. Work through it top to bottom:

  • Pick a piece — the top row of buttons chooses which equipped item to dye: Armor, Helmet or Cloak. The active piece is highlighted.
  • Pick a colour channel — the two rows below are the six tint channels: Cloth 1/2, Leather 1/2 and Metal 1/2. Each part of a piece uses one of these; the active channel is highlighted, and there is a separate palette for cloth/leather versus metal.
  • Pick a colour — click any swatch in the grid to apply it instantly to your character. There are 176 colours per channel; use Prev colors / More colors to page through them. The line above the grid names the current colour (e.g. Color #88 — Red).

The buttons along the bottom:

  • Revert — undo everything back to how the piece looked when you opened the window.
  • Reshape appearance… — switch to the model editor to change the shape of armor/cloak/shield/weapon parts (see Item Appearance Crafting).
  • Copy colors / Paste colors — copy a piece's full six-channel colour scheme, then paste it onto another piece so all your gear matches. Your copied scheme is saved to your character and stays copied across sessions and reboots (until you copy a different one).
  • Save and Close — keep your changes and close the window. (Your colours are already applied as you click, so simply closing keeps them.)

Shields & weapons: their colours are baked into the model rather than the six tint channels, so recolour or restyle them through Reshape appearance… rather than the swatch grid.

Weapon Renaming — Engraver's Chisel

The Engraver's Chisel lets you give your weapon a name of your own — turn Longsword +5 into Goblin-Cleaver or Sting. Renaming is purely cosmetic: it never changes an item's stats, and the new name is saved on the item itself, so it survives logouts and server reboots.

Where to get it: buy an Engraver's Chisel from the Well Mart in the Well of Eru for 25,000 gp. Each chisel is single-use — one chisel, one engraving.

How to use it:

  • Equip the weapon you want to rename in your main hand.
  • Use (activate) the chisel from your inventory.
  • Speak the new name in chat — your very next chat line becomes the weapon's new name. The line is not broadcast to other players.

Name rules: at most 40 characters, and the characters < and > are not allowed. Say cancel (or activate the chisel and change your mind with any invalid name) and the chisel is not consumed — only a successful engraving spends it.

Forge of Wonders — Item Enchanting

Four Forge of Wonders locations let you permanently add or replace magical properties on any equippable item, for a gold cost equal to the increase in the item's assessed value. Each forge is staffed by a smith NPC. Place exactly one item on the nearby anvil, then speak to the smith; pick the property you want and the smith quotes the cost before you confirm. When a property of the same type is already on the item it is replaced, not stacked.

Pricing is on the item's true value: the cost is always computed as if the item were fully identified and unrestricted — leaving an item unidentified (or any other trick that zeroes its sell value) no longer discounts the enchantment or slips past the value caps.

Each tier enforces a maximum item value and a maximum number of properties. Refusals are graceful: if an enchantment would push the item past either ceiling the smith says the item is already as fine as that forge can make it and no gold is charged. Forges only enchant an item upward — they never apply a change that lowers its assessed value, and replacing an existing property is always allowed even at the property cap (adding a new one past the cap is not). Each forge enforces only its own ceiling, so taking an item to a higher tier raises the limits.

Appraise raises the value ceiling. A high Appraise skill lets a forge bind more worth into a single item. The smith always lets you "take 20" (your Appraise skill + 20, no dice roll), and the extra headroom scales with that result up to a maximum of +500,000 gp at an Appraise check of 65 — reachable by a level-40 character with full Appraise ranks and a little Charisma or an Appraise item. You need at least one point of Appraise before any bonus applies. The bonus stacks on each tier's ceiling: a maxed appraiser can push a piece to 750,000 gp at Tagget's, 1,000,000 gp at Rivendell, and 1,250,000 gp at the Moria and Kallrist Crypt forges. The forge tells you its current ceiling and your remaining headroom before you spend.

Boss-kill forge bonuses. Slaying distinct bosses from the Roll of the Fallen (the boss board in the Well of Eru — solo or party credit both count, but killing the same boss twice does not) earns your character a personal bonus to the forge caps. The bonus is a percentage of each forge's own value ceiling, applied before any Appraise headroom, and the forge tells you your current tier when you work its anvil:

A full boss-slayer can therefore push a piece to 900,000 gp at Moria or the Kallrist Crypt (1,400,000 with maxed Appraise) with 7 enchantments. Gear forged with these bonuses is safe to trade: the contraband law judges every item against the maximum earnable ceiling, never against the holder's own progress.

Runes of Expansion — extra enchantment slots. A rare boss drop, the Rune of Expansion is a consumable that permanently widens a single item so a forge may bind one more enchantment into it than the law would otherwise allow. Activate the rune and target the item in your pack you want to expand; the rune is consumed and that item gains +1 property slot. Up to three runes may be bound into a single item (10 enchantments total on a fully-runed piece worked at a top-tier forge). The expansion is intrinsic to the item — it persists across sessions and travels with the item when you trade it, and the contraband law honors the extra slots so an expanded item is never seized for them. See the Rune of Expansion item page for where it can be obtained.

Disenchanting (no refunds): every forge smith offers "Strip an enchantment from the item on the anvil". You pick exactly one property and the smith destroys it permanently. The magic is simply lost — no gold is ever paid out for removed enchantments.

Kallrist Crypt — the Signature Wardings. Deep beneath the Kallrist Crypt, past the elemental-pillar riddle and the crypt guardian, Kalrûn the Deathless keeps a fourth forge. It works at the same top ceiling as Moria (6 properties, 750,000 gp), but it is a small, specialized forge: it does not offer the shared Low/Mid/High enchant menu at all. Its whole trade is the crypt's own signature work — protective wardings no other smith will bind, and a negative-energy edge drawn from the grave. Kalrûn will only wake for one who bears the Horn of the Fell Beast looted from the guardian — keep the horn in your pack or the anvil stays cold.

Custom Gameplay Items

Items that add mechanics the base game does not have.

Rod of Fast Buffing

Sold at the Well-Mart at the Well of Eru, the Rod of Fast Buffing lets casters batch their pre-combat buffs into a single action:

  • Store spells by casting buff spells at the rod — each one is remembered, in order.
  • Activate the rod (out of combat) to re-cast every stored spell on yourself, one after another, in one go.
  • The rod cannot be used in combat — it is a setup tool, not an in-fight panic button.

A big quality-of-life upgrade for buff-heavy casters who would otherwise click through a dozen spells before every fight. Based on the Rod of Fast Buffing prefab from the Neverwinter Vault.

Rod of Fast Lore

Also sold at the Well-Mart, the Rod of Fast Lore identifies a whole inventory at once instead of one item at a time:

  • Activate it on yourself (or on another player) to scan that character's inventory and attempt to identify every unidentified item in one pass.
  • Each item rolls a Lore check — your Lore skill + a d20 against a difficulty based on the item's value (gold value ÷ 100 + 1). Higher Lore identifies more, and more valuable items resist longer.
  • Your combat log lists what was identified and what failed (by item type), so you can re-try the failures after a Lore boost. When you scan another player, both of you see the results.

This is the same Lore rule the Party Loot Roll uses to identify unidentified drops.

Good/Evil Faction — Choosing Your Side

The module's in-game faction system is entirely separate from your character's D&D alignment.

Two player-facing factions govern which NPCs are hostile to you as you travel Middle-earth: Good (Gondor, Rivendell, Lothlórien, Helm's Deep, Edoras, …) and Evil (Mordor, Isengard, Cirith Ungol, Carn Dûm, …). See the Factions page for the full list and reputation matrix.

Alignment ≠ Faction. Your character's D&D alignment (Good / Neutral / Evil on the character sheet) has no effect on NPC hostility. It is cosmetic. All characters — regardless of alignment — choose a side via the orbs. A Neutral-aligned character picks Good or Evil the same as anyone else.

To change your character-sheet alignment, visit the Alignment Adjustment NPC in Bree at any time.

Choosing Your Side in the Well of Eru

Each time you are about to leave the Well of Eru through one of the teleporters, click one of the two orbs to set your faction for the session:

OrbEffect
Shaft of Light (blue/white) Declares you Good. Good-faction NPCs treat you as an ally; Evil-faction NPCs become hostile.
Shaft of Light — Red Declares you Evil. Evil-faction NPCs treat you as an ally; Good-faction NPCs become hostile.

After clicking the orb, use the Guardian of Light (Good) or Guardian of Darkness (Evil) to teleport to your destination. The teleporters each offer a list of faction-appropriate locations.

Your side is remembered — it is stored per character and re-applied automatically every time you log in. You only need to touch an orb again when you want to change sides.

Your Side Changes Which Encounters Spawn

Roaming encounters (the ambushes that appear as you travel, as opposed to NPCs standing where they are placed) are tied to a faction, and an encounter only fires for travellers that faction treats as an enemy. Most of the module's encounters — around 700 of them — belong to the generic Hostile faction and ambush everybody. But 77 encounters belong to the Good or Evil faction instead, and those stop spawning for players who have taken that side.

Nothing here is dynamic: an encounter's audience is fixed in the module, not chosen at spawn time. It just looks dynamic from the road, because your allegiance decides which half of the world is willing to ambush you.

Shapeshifting: Full Equipment Merge

Every player polymorph now merges all of your equipped items' magical properties into the new form — stock NWN merged only a per-form subset (druid Wild Shape: armor, helmet and shield only), and never put weapon properties on a form's natural attacks.

  • Weapon → claws, bite and slam. Your equipped weapon's properties are copied onto the form's natural attacks (and onto its right-hand weapon for humanoid forms). If you shift while unarmed, your gloves' properties ride the natural attacks instead.
  • Everything else → hide. Armor, helmet, shield, both rings, amulet, cloak, boots, belt and gloves merge onto the form's creature hide.
  • Applies to: druid Wild Shape and Elemental Shape, all Shifter shapes (Greater Wildshape I–IV, Humanoid, Undead, Dragon, Outsider, Construct), and the arcane self-polymorphs Polymorph Self, Shapechange and Tenser's Transformation (which merged nothing at all in stock NWN).

No stacking games: when several merged items carry the same property type (say, three AC bonuses), only the highest applies — the merge gives you the best of each property across your gear, not the sum. Form base stats are unchanged; your shifted damage is only ever as good as the weapon you bring.

Don't trust the character sheet while shifted: it keeps showing the form's own base attack and damage and never redraws for the merged bonuses. The combat log shows the real to-hit and damage — see Looks Like a Bug — Isn't.

Soul-fatigue on Heal

Healing is a timed resource, not a button you hold. Being healed by one of the three full-restore spells — Heal, Mass Heal or Greater Restoration, including from Heal potions and any item that casts them — leaves a stack of soul-fatigue on you. The order of events matters:

  1. The heal resolves in full first — you go to 100% HP as normal.
  2. You then take 8% of your maximum HP as damage for every stack you were already carrying, applied after the top-off so the heal cannot erase it — less whatever the healer's Heal skill shaves off (see below).
  3. One new stack is added.
  • Each stack decays on its own independent 3-minute timer. You are told on screen how many stacks you hold and when each one falls off.
  • Soul-fatigue is a debt you cannot walk away from. Stacks are saved to your character, so they survive a logout — and the timers pause while you are offline, so logging out to wait them down achieves nothing. Dying does not clear them either: you respawn carrying exactly what you had. The only thing that removes a stack is three minutes of being in the world.
  • A heal at zero stacks is completely free — full benefit, one stack, no damage. It is chain-healing that turns dangerous.
  • The damage is a scripted cost, not an attack: damage resistance, damage reduction and immunities do not reduce it. It can kill you — when the bill exceeds the hit points you have, it kills you outright rather than leaving you on 1 HP, and you get a red on-screen notice naming soul-fatigue as the cause. The on-screen warning after each heal tells you what the next one will cost, and simply says it will kill you once that is the honest answer.
  • Shapeshifting is not a way out. Wild shape, Greater Wild Shape, Shifter forms, Dragon Shape, Elemental Shape and Polymorph Self all carry their own, usually larger, pool of hit points — and the bill is charged against the form you are wearing. A dragon-shape druid pays 8% per stack of the dragon's maximum, not of their character sheet, so the percentages in the table below hold in every shape.
  • Casting Heal on undead harms them rather than healing them, so it produces no stack and no damage. DMs are unaffected.

The Heal skill is your mitigation — and this is what it is for at high level. The two mechanics shipped together and are meant to be answered with pacing and mitigation, not with spam and retreat. Design background: Endgame Difficulty — Soul-fatigue on Heal.

Doubled Spell & Effect Duration

Every temporary effect you or your companions create lasts twice as long as stock NWN — buffs you cast, potions and scrolls you drink, debuffs you inflict on enemies, and buffs from your henchmen, summons and familiars. Effects that enemies place on you are unchanged.

This stacks with the Extend Spell metamagic. Extend already doubles a spell's duration in its own right, so an Extended buff ends up four times the base duration on this server.

Exception — invisibility-type effects. Improved Invisibility, Invisibility, Invisibility Sphere, concealment, Sanctuary, Ethereal Visage and Etherealness keep their normal duration. These are applied as linked effects, and their "go visible but keep your concealment when you attack" behaviour depends on that engine link — which the duration-doubling system cannot safely rebuild — so they are left untouched. The exclusion is enforced both by spell id (so the whole spell is skipped before any re-timing, covering potions/wands/scrolls too) and by effect type (so invisibility applied directly by a script is caught as well).

Exception — Divine Might & Divine Shield. These Paladin feats also build their buff as a linked effect (attack+damage, or AC+visual), so they carry the same risk as the invisibility family above and are likewise left at their normal duration rather than doubled.

Character Progression

Predictable hit points and an extended level ceiling past the normal cap of 40.

Maximum Hit Points on Level-Up

Every character gains maximum hit points on every level-up, server-wide — no hit-die roll, no bad rolls. Your HP per level is always the top of your class hit die plus Constitution. (Set at module load via the administration max hit points play option.)

Maximum Ability Score Bonus from Items

The stock NWN cap on ability score bonuses from items and effects is +12. This server raises it to +24, allowing high-end gear and buffs to reach higher stat totals without hitting an invisible wall. (Set via ruleset.combat.max-ability-bonus in settings.tml.)

No Multiclass XP Penalty

Stock NWN quietly taxes multiclass characters: if your highest and lowest class levels ever differ by more than one, XP you earn is reduced until you level the lagging class back up. This server disables that penalty entirely — every class combination earns full XP, no matter how unevenly your levels are split. With up to 4 classes stackable per character, build experimentation is the point, so there is no XP cost for going wide instead of staying balanced.

Kill XP — How Much a Creature Is Worth

Experience from a kill depends on two things: the creature's Challenge Rating (CR) and your own level. Every character level raises the CR you need to fight for a full payout, so content you farmed happily at one level quietly stops being worth your time a few levels later — the game is pushing you onward to harder ground.

The cap is 6,000 XP per kill. Nothing pays more, at any level, from any creature. With a 2× premium boost active that becomes 12,000. A kill that falls far below your level pays a token 60 — but "far below" is measured against your level, so a brand-new character is paid properly for the rats, skeletons and goblins it is actually meant to be fighting: roughly 300 XP for the weakest creature in the game at level 1, and around 650 for a CR 1.

You are never penalised for punching above your weight. A low-level character who takes down something far above them is paid at least as much as a higher-level character would be for the identical kill — the award for a given CR never rises as you level.

The Full XP at CR column is the point where a kill pays the whole 6,000; anything at or above that CR pays the same. Levels between those shown follow the same curve.

Legendary Levels: 41–60

In development for Season 2. Levels 41–60 are being rebuilt as real character levels. The experience table below is final and already in the build; the level-cap lift and the per-class feat allotments are still being worked on, so the climb past 40 is not open yet. Progress is tracked on the Roadmap.

Casters: two things past level 40 look broken and are not — an empty spell-selection screen (see Spellbook Study) and feats listed twice on your sheet (see Why a feat can show twice).

Standard NWN stops at level 40. On this server, levels 41 to 60 are an extension of the game's own level progression rather than a bolt-on side system: you level up the ordinary way, at the ordinary spot on the character sheet, by earning the XP the table below asks for. There is no separate leveling NPC, no separate area to visit, and no second XP tally to keep track of — your XP bar simply keeps going past level 40.

Because they are real levels, each one is a level in a class you choose, and the usual rules for what a level gives you apply. Exactly which feats become selectable at these levels is still being finalised — see the Roadmap for the current state of that work.

Prestige class cap: 40, not 50. Any single prestige class can only be leveled up to 40 — still a big improvement over the stock limit of 30, but don't expect to push a prestige class all the way to the level 50 mark some players assume from the level 60 total. To reach the full legendary cap you'll need to spread your last levels across more than one class (prestige and/or base).

Spell slots at legendary levels

Stock NWN stops handing casters new spell slots at level 20 — a level 40 wizard has exactly as many slots per spell level as a level 20 one. That flat stretch does not continue through the legendary levels. Every fourth legendary level — 44, 48, 52, 56 and 60 — grants one extra slot at every spell level you can cast, on top of the caster level, save DC, duration and damage-cap scaling you were already getting.

Paladins and rangers advance on a gentler schedule — one extra slot at level 50 and another at 60 — because for them the legendary levels are mostly a martial payoff already. Bonus slots from a high spellcasting ability score stack on top of all of this exactly as they always have, and levels 1–40 are completely unchanged.

Already past level 40? You do not need to relevel. Slots are recalculated from your level whenever you rest, so log in, rest, and the new slots are simply there.

Spellbook Study — choosing spells past level 40

Casting more spells per day is what the table above changes. Choosing new spells is a separate matter, and past level 40 it runs into a hard limit in the game client itself — not in this server's rules. The level-up screen still asks a wizard to choose two spells, but only the cantrip tab has anything in it; every other spell-level tab greys out. There is nothing to pick, and the picks were spent anyway when you continued past the screen. Every avenue for repairing that screen was tried and none of it works — it is the client's own window, so no rules table, content pack or server script can reach it.

So the server hands the spells out itself. When a wizard with 41 or more wizard levels gains a level, a Spellbook Study window opens a few seconds after the level-up finishes, listing every arcane spell you do not yet know. Pick a spell level along the top, click Learn, and the spell goes straight into your spellbook.

  • Two spells per wizard level, exactly the entitlement the level-up screen was eating. Nothing more, nothing less.
  • Nothing you have already earned is lost. The first time you log in after this goes live, you are paid for every wizard level you gained past 40 while the screen was broken — a wizard 53 is owed 26 spells. It is paid once, and re-levelling cannot earn it twice.
  • You do not have to choose on the spot. Close the window and your picks are still banked. Finish a rest and it opens again; a login message tells you how many you still have waiting.
  • The usual rules still apply: you are only offered spells you can actually cast, never one you already know, and a specialist wizard is not offered its opposition school.
  • Scrolls still work too. Scribing from a scroll is unaffected and costs you none of these picks.

Still to come: sorcerers and bards. For now, treat your repertoire as locked in at level 40. You keep every spell you know and gain more casts per day of all of them, but you cannot yet swap a known spell for a better one on level-up. That swap is the next piece of this work — watch the Roadmap. Nothing is being lost in the meantime: sorcerers and bards gain no new spells known past 40 under the stock rules either, so there are no picks quietly going missing.

Clerics, druids, paladins and rangers are unaffected — they know their entire class list automatically, so there was never anything to choose. Multiclass casters are unaffected below 40: the limit only ever applied to a class you have 40 or more levels in, and taking a caster class up from a low level at character level 41+ has always worked normally.

— Caster feats at legendary levels

The same level-40 blind spot that ate the spell picker also ate most of a caster's feat list. Once you had 41 or more levels in a casting class, every feat that requires you to be able to cast spells of a certain level quietly disappeared from both your class bonus feat list and your general feat pick. A pure wizard at 47 was offered exactly three feats: Brew Potion, Craft Wand and Epic Spell. Everything else — Spell Focus, Silent Spell, Automatic Still Spell, Automatic Quicken Spell, Epic Spell Focus — was simply not on the list.

Those feats are back. They are published a second time as class-specific entries that the level-up screen can see past level 40, and picking one gives you the real feat. Every requirement is unchanged: Automatic Still Spell still wants Still Spell and 27 ranks of Spellcraft, Epic Spell Focus still wants Greater Spell Focus in that school. Nothing became easier to get — it became possible to get.

Why a feat can show twice

A caster at class level 40 or above will see some feats listed twice on the character sheet, under the same name. A level 48 wizard, for instance, shows Extend Spell, Still Spell and Combat Casting twice each. This is expected and there is nothing to fix.

One of the pair is the real feat. The other is an inert stand-in — the replacement entry described above, which carries the real feat's own name and description rather than a made-up one. Holding both is the point:

  • It is not a double benefit. The effect applies once. Nothing stacks, and nothing is being counted twice.
  • It did not cost you a second pick. You spent one feat slot, exactly as you meant to.
  • It is what stops you wasting a pick. The level-up screen never offers a feat you already hold — so by giving you both halves, the server makes sure neither half is ever dangled in front of you again.
  • There is nothing to do about it. No action, no cleanup, and it cannot be turned off.

It can appear on its own, without you picking anything. If you already had one of these feats from below level 40, the matching stand-in is added the moment that class reaches 40 — so a duplicate can turn up at a level-up or a login where you chose nothing at all. That is the pairing being applied in the other direction, and it is the whole reason the level-up screen stops offering you feats you own.

Below class level 40 none of this exists. A level 1 wizard or sorcerer sees each feat exactly once, and levels 1–40 pick from exactly the list they always did.

How far each class reaches

A feat that demands you cast spells of a given level is still barred if your class never gets there — that is the original rule, not a new restriction. It is why a bard sees fewer replacement entries than a wizard, and no Epic Spell Focus at all.

ClassHighest spell levelWhat that means
Wizard, Sorcerer, Cleric, Druid9thThe full set, including Epic Spell Focus and the Automatic Still / Silent / Quicken Spell chains.
Bard6thSpell Focus and the metamagic feats. No Epic Spell Focus, no Automatic metamagic — both need 9th-level spells.
Paladin, Ranger4thThe feats that ask for 4th-level spells or less.

This keys on your class level, not your caster level. The replacement entries unlock when your wizard (or cleric, or druid…) level itself reaches 41. Caster levels granted by a prestige class do not count toward that number, and no amount of prestige levelling brings the entries forward.

Pale Master is the only class in the game that advances spellcasting at all — one arcane caster level for every two Pale Master levels. It will happily let you cast higher-level spells sooner than your wizard level alone would, and the ordinary feats below 41 respect that exactly as they always have. What it will not do is unlock the replacement entries early. Dragon Disciple grants no spellcasting advancement whatsoever in NWN, so it has no bearing here in either direction.

In practice this costs nobody a feat. The client fault these entries work around also only begins once a casting class passes 40 — below that the ordinary feats are offered normally. The rule is worth knowing so you build with it in mind, not because there is a trap in it.

Monk Weapons

Three weapons have been reclassified for monks relative to stock NWN:

  • Shuriken — counts as a monk weapon; also benefits from Weapon Finesse.
  • Quarterstaff — counts as a monk weapon; also benefits from Weapon Finesse.
  • Katana — counts as a monk weapon, but still requires Exotic Weapon Proficiency; does not benefit from Weapon Finesse.

Critical Hits & Devastating Critical

A devastating critical no longer kills outright. The stock save-or-die is gone — for melee weapons, ranged weapons, and unarmed and creature attacks alike. In its place, a devastating critical lands as a very large hit: three extra dice of damage, of the same damage type your weapon already deals, scaled to the weapon you are swinging.

Weapon sizeExtra dieDevastating CriticalWith Legendary Butcher
Small (dagger, shuriken, unarmed…)d6+3d6+8d6
Medium (longsword, mace…)d8+3d8+8d8
Large (greatsword, halberd…)d10+3d10+8d10

This cuts both ways. The rule is symmetric — it applies to every creature in the module exactly as it applies to you. No more losing a level 60 character to one lucky swing from a random mob, and no more deleting a boss with one. Nothing else about the feat changed: same threat range, same prerequisites.

Legendary Butcher — a level 60 Legendary Feat for those who want to lean into critting. It adds five extra dice on any critical hit, not just a devastating one, using the same size scale, and it stacks with the devastating-critical dice. A large weapon in the hands of someone with both lands +8d10. It requires Devastating Critical with some weapon.

Two things worth knowing:

  • The extra damage is typed physical (piercing/slashing/bludgeoning, matching your weapon), on purpose — so heavy damage reduction and physical immunity eat part of it, just like the rest of your weapon damage.
  • The combat log no longer announces "devastating critical" by name. Taking the instant kill out at the root also took the message with it. The hit shows as an ordinary critical, with the extra dice rolled into its damage.

Immunity to critical hits is no longer the default on tough enemies. It went out with the same change — once a devastating critical stopped being an instant kill, there was no reason to keep blanket immunity on everything dangerous. It now survives only where it makes thematic sense: undead, and the Nazgûl, plus a small hand-picked set of bosses. A critical build is a viable build against most of the module.

Death, Dying & Respawn

A bleed-out window when you drop, and a light respawn penalty with no XP loss.

Bleeding Out

When you fall to 0 HP or below you begin bleeding rather than dying instantly. You lose 1 HP every round (6 seconds) and die at −20 HP (twice the vanilla −10 threshold), giving allies a longer window to reach you. Each bleeding round there is a 10% chance to stabilize; once stabilized you begin slowly healing back instead of bleeding. (Expect the usual pained voice-lines while you are down.)

You Cannot Log Out of Death

Logging out and back in does not restore health or spells — and it does not escape death. If you disconnect while dead or bleeding, you are still dead or bleeding when you return; your state is saved across the logout, so there is no relog trick to recover.

Respawn Penalty

Pressing the respawn button returns you to your bind point with:

  • 5 HP (not the vanilla 1 HP), then a full effect clear.
  • No XP loss — the XP penalty is disabled entirely.
  • A 10% gold penalty — that gold is dropped at your place of death in a bag that anyone can loot, so a quick recovery run can get it back.
