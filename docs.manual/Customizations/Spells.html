Spell Changes

Player-cast spells and effects last twice as long, epic spells are dramatically stronger than stock NWN, a few utility spells are re-tuned, and one is disabled outright.

The epic spells have been rebuilt to feel genuinely epic. Several summon and ward effects cannot be dispelled.

Base duration is 15 rounds — the Lingering Song feat adds +15 rounds, and Lasting Impression multiplies the duration ×15.

Bard Song Ladder (Bard)

Stock Bard Song stopped scaling at Bard 20 and gave only modest bonuses. Here it is the mirror of Curse Song — a ladder of 23 tiers reached only when you meet both its Perform rank and its Bard level, granting every bonus on that row to the bard and allies in a 30 ft sphere. Duration rules are identical to Curse Song. Because buffing a whole party is stronger than debuffing enemies, the bonuses are tuned conservatively (roughly half of Curse Song's combat magnitudes) and the sonic-damage column becomes temporary hit points here.

Shapeshifting: Full Equipment Merge

Every player polymorph now merges all of your equipped items' magical properties into the new form — stock NWN merged only a per-form subset (druid Wild Shape: armor, helmet and shield only), and never put weapon properties on a form's natural attacks.

  • Weapon → claws, bite and slam. Your equipped weapon's properties are copied onto the form's natural attacks (and onto its right-hand weapon for humanoid forms). If you shift while unarmed, your gloves' properties ride the natural attacks instead.
  • Everything else → hide. Armor, helmet, shield, both rings, amulet, cloak, boots, belt and gloves merge onto the form's creature hide.
  • Applies to: druid Wild Shape and Elemental Shape, all Shifter shapes (Greater Wildshape I–IV, Humanoid, Undead, Dragon, Outsider, Construct), and the arcane self-polymorphs Polymorph Self, Shapechange and Tenser's Transformation (which merged nothing at all in stock NWN).

No stacking games: when several merged items carry the same property type (say, three AC bonuses), only the highest applies — the merge gives you the best of each property across your gear, not the sum. Form base stats are unchanged; your shifted damage is only ever as good as the weapon you bring.

Don't trust the character sheet while shifted: it keeps showing the form's own base attack and damage and never redraws for the merged bonuses. The combat log shows the real to-hit and damage — see Looks Like a Bug — Isn't.

You can use items while shifted. Stock NWN locks your inventory the moment you polymorph. Here you can still drink potions, read scrolls and use other items from your pack in any form.

Scaled Summons & Animal Companions

Your summoned creatures and animal companion grow with you. The moment one appears it gains a single permanent, undispellable boost based on your investment as a summoner — so a high-level druid's or ranger's pet stays relevant on this high-magic server instead of falling behind. The bonus scales with your class level (druid, or the higher of druid/ranger for an animal companion), your Animal Empathy skill, and the Conjuration spell-focus feat line (Spell Focus → Greater → Epic Spell Focus). Summons from other classes (e.g. a wizard's) are unaffected.

An epic summon and a normal summon at the same time

Stock NWN lets you have one summoned creature at a time, so casting Mummy Dust or Dragon Knight sent your Summon Creature away. Here the two epic summons take a different slot, so you can have one epic summon out alongside one normal summon. Mummy Dust and Dragon Knight still replace each other, and normal summons are still one at a time.

Other Spell Changes

  • Summon Creature I–IX — fixed duration of 24 hours; the duration no longer scales with caster level. (The summoned creature's stats still scale with you — see Scaled Summons & Animal Companions above.)
  • Negative Energy Burst — the bonus damage is your full caster level (d8 + caster level). Stock NWN caps that bonus at +20.

Time Stop is disabled. It cannot be cast on this server — attempting to cast it does nothing.

Attack Bonuses from Spells Stack

In stock NWN, attack bonuses from spells and effects don't add up: the game keeps only the single largest one. A permanent +5 from a feat would make a +2 from War Cry worth nothing at all.

Here these sources are added together instead:

  • the spells Bless, Aid, Prayer, War Cry, Divine Favor, Divine Power and the Divine Champion's Divine Wrath;
  • Bard Song;
  • legendary feats such as Legendary Prowess, Legendary Grip and Legendary Reaping.

Bard Song's and the legendary feats' damage bonuses stack the same way. Your weapon's enchantment adds on top, as it always has. Stock spells not listed here still follow the largest-only rule. Your total attack bonus is still capped, but the cap is higher here — see Maximum Attack Bonus.

Your Companions' Area Spells Don't Hit You

This server runs on Hardcore rules, where area spells hurt everyone in the blast, including your friends. A companion's Hellball could flatten the player who summoned it.

Now, when a player's henchman, summon, familiar or animal companion casts an area spell (Hellball, Fireball, Meteor Swarm, Ice Storm, Cone of Cold, Firestorm, Horrid Wilting, Wail of the Banshee, Implosion, Blade Barrier, Creeping Doom and the like), it can't hurt you, anyone in your party, or your party's pets and summons. It still hurts enemies normally.

This only works one way. Your own area spells still hit your henchmen and party, exactly as in stock Hardcore rules, so watch where you aim.

Soul-fatigue on Heal

Healing is a timed resource, not a button you hold. Being healed by one of the three full-restore spells — Heal, Mass Heal or Greater Restoration, including from Heal potions and any item that casts them — leaves a stack of soul-fatigue on you. The order of events matters:

  1. The heal resolves in full first — you go to 100% HP as normal.
  2. You then take 8% of your maximum HP as damage for every stack you were already carrying, applied after the top-off so the heal cannot erase it — less whatever the healer's Heal skill shaves off (see below).
  3. One new stack is added.
  • Each stack decays on its own independent 3-minute timer. You are told on screen how many stacks you hold and when each one falls off.
  • Soul-fatigue is a debt you cannot walk away from. Stacks are saved to your character, so they survive a logout — and the timers pause while you are offline, so logging out to wait them down achieves nothing. Dying does not clear them either: you respawn carrying exactly what you had. Three minutes of being in the world removes a stack — and the one and only shortcut is a pipe of Shire leaf smoked at the Smoking Chamber, which clears the lot at once.
  • A heal at zero stacks is completely free — full benefit, one stack, no damage. It is chain-healing that turns dangerous.
  • The damage is a scripted cost, not an attack: damage resistance, damage reduction and immunities do not reduce it. It can kill you — when the bill exceeds the hit points you have, it kills you outright rather than leaving you on 1 HP, and you get a red on-screen notice naming soul-fatigue as the cause. The on-screen warning after each heal tells you what the next one will cost, and simply says it will kill you once that is the honest answer.
  • Shapeshifting is not a way out. Wild shape, Greater Wild Shape, Shifter forms, Dragon Shape, Elemental Shape and Polymorph Self all carry their own, usually larger, pool of hit points — and the bill is charged against the form you are wearing. A dragon-shape druid pays 8% per stack of the dragon's maximum, not of their character sheet, so the percentages in the table below hold in every shape.
  • Casting Heal on undead harms them rather than healing them, so it produces no stack and no damage. DMs are unaffected.

The Heal skill is your mitigation — and this is what it is for at high level. The caster's total Heal skill (ranks plus Wisdom bonus plus item bonuses — the number on your character sheet, not just the ranks you bought) reduces the soul-fatigue damage by 0.5% per point, counting up to 60 points — a maximum 30% off. Drinking your own potion or healing yourself counts your own skill, so the investment protects you whether or not you are the party's healer.

At the 60-point cap each stack costs 5.6% of your maximum HP instead of 8%. In concrete terms: chain-healing kills you on the 14th heal inside one three-minute window with no Heal skill, and on the 19th at the cap. The on-screen message names the reduction every time it applies, so you can see the skill working.

Don't plan on backing off to wait it out. Against the great bosses, walking away from the fight triggers Boss Enrage on Retreat — the boss heals and grows stronger while your stacks tick down. The two mechanics shipped together and are meant to be answered with pacing and mitigation, not with spam and retreat. Design background: Endgame Difficulty — Soul-fatigue on Heal.

Doubled Spell & Effect Duration

Every temporary effect you or your companions create lasts twice as long as stock NWN — buffs you cast, potions and scrolls you drink, debuffs you inflict on enemies, and buffs from your henchmen, summons and familiars. Effects that enemies place on you are unchanged.

This stacks with the Extend Spell metamagic. Extend already doubles a spell's duration in its own right, so an Extended buff ends up four times the base duration on this server.

Exception — invisibility-type effects. Improved Invisibility, Invisibility, Invisibility Sphere, concealment, Sanctuary, Ethereal Visage and Etherealness keep their normal duration. These are applied as linked effects, and their "go visible but keep your concealment when you attack" behaviour depends on that engine link — which the duration-doubling system cannot safely rebuild — so they are left untouched. The exclusion is enforced both by spell id (so the whole spell is skipped before any re-timing, covering potions/wands/scrolls too) and by effect type (so invisibility applied directly by a script is caught as well).

Exception — Divine Might & Divine Shield. These Paladin feats also build their buff as a linked effect (attack+damage, or AC+visual), so they carry the same risk as the invisibility family above and are likewise left at their normal duration rather than doubled.
